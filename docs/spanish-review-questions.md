# Eight questions for the bilingual staff

A native-speaker editor went through the Spanish side of mercermedtech.com and
fixed everything that was clearly wrong. These eight are the ones only the
people who answer the phone can settle, because they are about the word a
caller actually uses.

The site already uses the wording in the **We changed it to** line, so nothing
is waiting on an answer. If an answer comes back different, we change it back,
and the answer gets written into `design.md` section 15 so nobody has to ask
twice.

---

**1. The name of the computer program.**

- Site said: Capacitación en Alfabetización Digital
- We changed it to: Capacitación en Habilidades Digitales (Digital Literacy Training)

When you describe this program to a Spanish-speaking caller, what do you say?
Does anyone hear "alfabetización" and think it is a class for people who cannot
read? Does "Habilidades Digitales" describe it just as well? If NJDOL requires a
specific Spanish name for the program, tell us and we use theirs.

---

**2. Hands-on.**

- Site said: práctica con las manos
- We changed it to: mucha práctica / práctica con equipo real / Todas son prácticas

How do you say, in Spanish, that the classes are hands-on and not just reading?
We want the words you use on the phone.

---

**3. The person at the county.**

- Site said: trabajador social (and the nav said "Para trabajadores sociales")
- We changed it to: trabajador de caso (caseworker), and the nav says "Para agencias"

When a caller mentions the person at the county who handles their TANF or SNAP,
what do they call that person? "Trabajador social", "trabajador de caso",
"manejador de casos", something else?

---

**4. "You are ready to train and look for work."**

- Site said: Está listo para capacitarse y buscar trabajo
- We changed it to: Quiere capacitarse y buscar trabajo

"Está listo" only fits a man, so a woman reading the eligibility list sees the
wrong word. Does "Quiere capacitarse y buscar trabajo" mean the same thing to a
caller, or does it lose the "ready to start now" part?

---

**5. Mouse.**

- Site said: el ratón
- We changed it to: el mouse

When you are showing someone the computer, do you say "el mouse" or "el ratón"?

---

**6. Medication Aide.**

- Site said: Asistente de Medicamentos Certificado
- We changed it to: Asistente Certificado de Medicamentos (Certified Medication Aide)

Do callers say "Asistente de Medicamentos", or something else? And does the
certificate the state sends have a Spanish name printed on it? We want the site
to match the certificate.

---

**7. The waitlist button.**

- Site said: Apúnteme en la lista
- We changed it to: Anóteme en la lista

For a program that has not opened yet, which one sounds more like you?

---

**8. The 100-hour placement.**

- Site says: pasantía, written "pasantía (externship)" the first time on a page
- Also considered: prácticas, or just saying "externship" in English

For the placement in the Medical Assistant and EKG programs, what do you say?
Whatever you say, we use it everywhere, and we can keep the English in
parentheses the first time.

---

**Settled, no question needed.** The reviewer also asked whether the photo in
"Sobre Mercer Med Tech" shows a woman, because the Spanish alt text says "Una
instructora" while the English says the genderless "An instructor". It does, so
the Spanish wording stays.
