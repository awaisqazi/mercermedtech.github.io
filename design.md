# Mercer Med Tech — Brand Identity & Design System

A reference for anyone designing, writing, or building for mercermedtech.com.
The goal is a high-trust, modern, approachable experience that feels closer to a
healthcare-tech product than a traditional vocational school site.

---

## 1. Brand Essence

**Who we are.** Mercer Med Tech, Inc. (MMT) is a state-approved private
vocational school in Lawrenceville, NJ, licensed by the New Jersey Department
of Labor & Workforce Development. We train people for the two kinds of jobs
central New Jersey is hiring for: care and computer work. The **Med** track is
health care certification, taught in small classes with hands-on practice, and
an externship in the programs that include one. The **Tech** track is computer
skills and help finding work, which right now means Digital Literacy Training,
delivered with NJDOL. Say what each program actually includes in that
program's own card and modal; never make "clinical experience" a blanket claim
for the Med track, and never name "skilled nursing facilities". See section 1a
for how the two tracks are named, coloured, and written about.

**Personality.** Warm, confident, modern, practical. We sound like a great
academic advisor: knowledgeable but never stiff, encouraging but never hype-y.

**Promise.** Real instructors, real skills, real careers. No fluff.

### Brand pillars

1. **Practical mastery.** Every program is hands-on and credential-ready.
2. **Personal attention.** Small class sizes, mentor-style faculty.
3. **Career launchpad.** We exist to get students into jobs, not into more debt.
4. **Modern medicine, modern training.** Clean tech, current data, BLS-backed
   outcomes.
5. **Accessible support.** Bilingual (English/Spanish) staff, help finding
   work, an accessible facility, and this sentence where a caseworker or a
   funded student will look: "Mercer Med Tech is on New Jersey's Eligible
   Training Provider List (ETPL)." Never claim a specific program is
   ETPL-listed, and never claim evening classes. Class times depend on the
   program, and the honest line is "Ask us and we will tell you the days and
   hours for the one you are looking at."

### Voice & tone

- **Do:** short sentences, plain words, direct verbs. "Hands-on training."
  "Talk to an advisor." "Seats are limited."
- **Don't:** corporate jargon, exclamation marks, hype, em-dashes, three-word
  alliterative slogans.
- **Pronouns.** "You" addresses the prospective student. "We" is MMT. Avoid
  third-person business-speak ("the institution provides...").
- **Numbers.** Keep them concrete and cited (BLS, NHA, school catalog). Never
  invent statistics.
- **Punctuation.** No em-dashes (`—`) or en-dashes (`–`). Use commas, periods,
  parentheses, or "to" as appropriate. Use the Oxford comma.

### Writing for this reader

**1. Respect first. This rule outranks every other line in this section.**
Never presume the reader lacks skills, experience, or intelligence. These are
adults with work and life experience who are adding a skill set. Do not write
"even if you have never...", "no experience needed", "don't worry", "it's
easy", or "simple" as a label for the person. Frame the skills check as
placing each person at the right level, never as reassurance for beginners.
Warm and relatable means direct, specific, and adult, not soothing.

> Rejected: "You do not need to know anything about computers to start."
> Preferred: "Digital Literacy Training prepares you for work that uses a
> computer every day. We start with a short skills check so your training fits
> what you already know."

**2. Who we are writing for.** Most people reading this site are adults on
TANF or SNAP in central New Jersey. Many have been out of work a while, many
are reading on a phone, and many read English as a second language. A smaller
group are county caseworkers deciding whether to send someone to us. Write for
the first group; the second group reads the same page and is reassured by the
same plainness. Write like the person at the front desk who is good at their
job: straight, warm, never talking down, never chirpy. Say what happens, in
what order, and what it costs.

**3. Do and don't, drawn from this site.**

| Don't | Do |
| --- | --- |
| "Investment in Your Career" | "What each program costs" |
| "we are committed to helping you navigate your funding options" | "We can show you how to apply." |
| "Comprehensive course covering clinical and administrative duties" | "You learn the clinical side and the office side." |
| "Personal attention from experienced instructors." | "Small classes. You get time with the instructor." |
| "Job placement help" | "Help finding work" |
| "Learn More" (nine times, three destinations) | "See the details", "Read about Digital Literacy Training" |
| "Start Your Application" (there is no application) | "Ask us to call you" |
| "real instructors, real outcomes, and the support you need to succeed" | "Real instructors. Small classes. Someone who picks up the phone." |
| "This growth is expected to result in approximately 112,300 job openings each year" | "The Bureau of Labor Statistics counts about 112,300 openings a year." |
| "A small, hands-on team committed to your success." | "The people you will actually deal with." |

**4. Banned words and constructions.** Never publish: empower, unlock,
journey, seamless, comprehensive, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, premier,
thrive, elevate, tailored, robust, leverage, synergy, holistic, transformative,
"navigate your options", "we are committed to", "designed to", "proudly",
"valuable", "dynamic opportunities", "in today's world", "look no further".
(The single allowed exception is "comprehensive personal care homes", which is
the New Jersey facility category, not an adjective we chose.)

Never use these shapes:

- **Rule-of-three lists.** Two items, or four. Three is the AI cadence.
- **"Whether you're X or Y."**
- **"Not just X, but Y."**
- **Stacked adjectives.** "valuable hands-on clinical externship experience" is
  one noun and four decisions you did not make.
- **Vague superlatives.** "leading", "premier", "best-in-class".
- **Headline case in the source string.** Write "What it costs", not "What It
  Costs". Buttons are sentence case too.
- **Em-dashes, en-dashes, and exclamation marks.** Repeated here because they
  keep coming back.

**5. Standing wording decisions.**

- **One phrase for the cost claim, everywhere: "No cost if you qualify."**
- **"Health care" as two words** in anything a person reads. "Allied health"
  stays in the catalog and the JSON-LD only.
- **"Help finding work" or "career services", never "placement".**
- **"National Healthcareer Association"** is the vendor's correct name.
- Name the source inside the sentence for every number: "The Bureau of Labor
  Statistics expects...".
- The NJDOL funding sentence is verbatim, everywhere it appears.
- **Contact routing: forms first, the phone number as an option.** On the
  homepage and the Digital Literacy Training page the phone number appears
  in the footer and once in the main contact section, nowhere else: no phone
  buttons, no floating call button, no "call us" in steps, FAQ answers, or
  form confirmations. Point people to the Digital Literacy interest form
  (`#interest`, or `#dlt-interest` on the homepage) or the contact form, and
  offer email as the alternative. Med program pages may still say "Call for
  the next start date".

### Source-of-truth hierarchy

When facts conflict, trust sources in this order:

1. The current school **catalog** (programs, hours, tuition, calendar, policies).
2. **Google Business Profile** (address, phone, hours as displayed in Maps).
3. **BLS / NHA** (salary expectations and outlook).
4. The **website** (which should reflect 1–3, not contradict them).

## 1a. Brand architecture: Med + Tech

The name carries the positioning. **Mercer Med Tech = Med and Tech**, two
training tracks under one school. The legal, licensed name stays "Mercer Med
Tech" everywhere: title tags, JSON-LD `name`, footer copyright, and every
licensing statement. Do not rename the school and do not edit the logo files.

### The two tracks

| Track    | What it is                                  | Programs                                                                                         |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Med**  | Allied health certification                 | Medical Assistant, Phlebotomy, PCT, EKG, CNA, Medication Aide, plus the two "coming soon" cards.   |
| **Tech** | Digital skills and career readiness         | Digital Literacy Training (no cost if eligible, NJDOL funded). Future Tech programs slot into the right column. |

Name them the same way in every surface: nav, section headings, tuition table
groups, enrollment cards, and the contact form's `<optgroup>` labels.

### Order rule

**Order rule: Med first/left, Tech second/right, everywhere, mirroring the
name.** Side by side, Med takes the left column. Stacked (mobile, or any
vertical list), Med comes first. This governs the hero track panels, the
enrollment cards, the two programs columns, the tuition table groups, the
contact form `<optgroup>`s, nav and footer links, the JSON-LD OfferCatalog,
and any prose that names both tracks. Never flip it with CSS `order`,
`row-reverse`, or grid placement at a breakpoint. The single exception is the
homepage announcement strip, which is about Digital Literacy on its own.

### Color ownership

Med keeps the brand teal family (`--med`, `--med-ink`, `--med-deep`). Tech
owns the indigo family (`--tech`, `--tech-ink`, `--tech-deep`,
`--tech-tint`). All tokens and their contrast ratios are in
`src/styles/tokens.css` and section 1b. A Tech-track element never uses
teal, and a Med-track element never uses indigo.

### Lockup and the split mark

The old 135deg teal-to-indigo `--lockup-gradient` is retired; the variable
now points at `--mark-split`, the split hairline used only at the top of the
header and footer (section 1b). There is no gradient text. As an eyebrow the
lockup sits inside the small bracket, teal left arm, indigo right arm.

The wordmark is CSS text, never an image: `.medtech-lockup` renders "Med" in
teal, a thin divider, and "Tech" in indigo, in Plus Jakarta Sans 800. It
appears in the hero eyebrow, the programs section header, and the footer
brand line.

**Positioning line:** "Med and Tech. Two paths to a better job." Use it in the
hero, meta copy, and the footer. Keep it within the voice rules in section 1.

### Program status

A program that is not currently enrolling stays on the site as a normal card
with the line **"Next sessions will be announced soon."** It is never listed as
enrolling and never shows a start date. Do not use "not available at this time"
and do not use "Contact us for more information" for these programs.

**The exact status strings, and nothing else:**

| Program | Status string |
| --- | --- |
| Digital Literacy Training | "Enrolling soon." (never "now enrolling", never a pulsing live badge). Badge: "Enrolling soon · No cost if you qualify." |
| Certified Medical Assistant | "New groups start regularly. The most recent group began September 14, 2026. Call for the next start date." |
| Phlebotomy Technician, Patient Care Technician | "Call for the next start date." |
| EKG Technician | "Call for availability." |
| CNA, Certified Medication Aide | "Next sessions will be announced soon." |
| Information sessions, anywhere | "Details on upcoming information sessions will be shared soon." No day, no time, no walk-in instruction, and no "what to bring". |

In practice that means: the normal card style (no muted or dashed treatment),
its usual hours badge, a `.program-contact-line` under the description, and its
usual "See the details" modal trigger. The modal opens with a
`.modal-contact-note` paragraph ("Next sessions will be announced soon.") and
an "Ask us about [program]" button that closes the modal and goes to
`#contact`. Keep it out of every "now enrolling" list: hero chips, enrollment
pills, meta and OG copy, and the JSON-LD `OfferCatalog`. It may appear in a
neutral list of programs (keywords, the contact form, the tuition table). Its
tuition row carries no status line.

### NJDOL rule (non-negotiable)

NJDOL is affiliated with Digital Literacy Training only. Therefore:

- Digital Literacy materials, including `digital-literacy.html`, never advertise
  MMT's healthcare or other programs. No program cards, no tuition table, no
  cross-sell. A plain "Mercer Med Tech home" link is the only outbound site link.
- Every Digital Literacy surface carries this sentence verbatim: "The funding for
  this initiative is being provided by the New Jersey Department of Labor and
  Workforce Development."
- Never mention stipends or payments to participants, and never promise a free
  laptop. Say technology access and other supports **may be available based on
  individual need**.

## 1b. Visual language: "Two paths"

The site is a poster system with a course-catalogue backbone. Type and colour
carry the identity, and a few small photos of the campus and the class site
sit beside the type as proof of place: the two brand colours as full-bleed
blocks, a display serif for headlines, the bracket from
the logo as the one signature motif, and programs, schedules and prices set
like a printed catalogue. These rules outrank any older component note
further down; when the two disagree, this section wins.

### Colour as structure

- **Paper and ink.** Neutral sections are warm paper (`--paper` #F6F3EE)
  with near-black ink (`--ink` #161A21, 15.8:1). The page is never pure
  white; white (`--white`) is only the inside of form fields.
- **Med blocks** are deep teal (`--med-deep` #0B5F5A) with white type
  (7.5:1). Accent on the block (numerals, brackets, status):
  `--med-on-deep` #8FE3DA (5.1:1). Supporting text: `--med-muted-on-deep`
  #CFE7E4 (5.8:1). The brand teal `--med` #00A69C is only 2.7:1 on paper, so
  on light backgrounds it is for rules and large brackets only; teal text on
  paper is `--med-ink` #006B64 (5.7:1).
- **Tech blocks** are deep indigo (`--tech-deep` #243B9E) with white type
  (9.6:1). Accent: `--tech-on-deep` #B4C3FF (5.6:1); supporting text
  `--tech-muted-on-deep` #D5DCF7 (7.0:1). On paper the indigo accent is
  `--tech` #3B5BDB (5.1:1) and indigo text `--tech-ink` #2B44A8 (7.6:1).
  `--tech-tint` #E6E9F5 is the indigo-tinted panel on Tech pages.
- **How a block works.** `.block-med` / `.block-tech` set the background and
  redefine the semantic tokens (`--text-main`, `--text-muted`, `--rule`,
  `--rule-strong`, `--border`, `--bracket`, `--accent-text`), so any
  component inside turns light by itself. `.panel-paper` inside a block puts
  paper and ink back, for forms and dense reading. Inside a block the solid
  button is paper with the block colour as text; the outline button is white.
- **Rhythm.** Paper, colour block, paper, down the page, so the structure is
  visible while scrolling on a phone. Home: hero headline on paper, the Med
  and Tech path blocks, enrollment and About on paper, the Med catalogue on
  paper, the Tech track block (indigo, interest form on paper inside), tuition
  on paper, Why Mercer Med Tech (teal, admissions on paper inside), grants on
  paper with an indigo grant panel, people and organizations on paper, the
  contact section (teal, form on paper inside), footer on ink. Digital
  Literacy page (Tech only, so no teal): hero indigo, information sessions on
  paper, interest form indigo, who qualifies on paper, what you learn on the
  indigo tint, outcomes on paper, support indigo, how to enroll on paper,
  partners on the tint, FAQ on paper, contact indigo, footer on ink.
- **Order rule still holds.** Med always first / left, Tech second / right.
  School-level blocks that are not about one track (contact, Why) use the
  school's brand teal; a Tech-only surface never uses teal.
- **No gradients, no glass, no shadows.** Section spacing is one fluid value,
  `--section-space` = `clamp(3rem, 8vw, 6rem)`.

### Typography

- **Fraunces** (Google Fonts, variable: optical size 9 to 144, weight 500 to
  700, SOFT, WONK) for H1 to H3, catalogue entries, numerals and the hero
  facts on the Digital Literacy page. `font-optical-sizing: auto`, weight
  600, `SOFT 30`. The H1 adds a little softness and the leaning "wonky"
  letters (`SOFT 60, WONK 1`).
- **Plus Jakarta Sans** (400 to 800) for body, UI, labels, H4 to H6, buttons
  and tables.
- One stylesheet request for both families, `display=swap`, preloaded in
  `BaseLayout.astro`.
- Scale: H1 `clamp(2.6rem, 9vw, 6rem)`, line-height 0.98, -0.025em. Section
  H2 `clamp(2.1rem, 5.2vw, 3.6rem)`, 1.02. Body 1rem / 1.6, lead 1.125 to
  1.1875rem. Headlines are left-aligned with `text-wrap: balance`.

### The bracket: the signature motif

The logo mark is a bracket, so the bracket is the one motif. It is drawn in
CSS (two three-sided boxes), never typed as a character, in the accent of the
section it sits in.

- **Framed statement** (`.bracketed`): at most one per section. Used on the
  key headline or promise: "[Two paths]" in the home H1 (`.bracket-pair`:
  teal left arm, indigo right arm), "[No cost if you qualify.]" in the
  Digital Literacy hero, the interest-form headings, "Who qualifies", "What
  you learn", the grant-funded panel, the contact heading. The frame hugs
  the words in CSS alone, with no script: the left arm opens the first line
  and the right arm is a zero-width inline box after the last word. On one
  line that is the whole frame; when the headline wraps, the arms open the
  first line and close the last, like a quotation, so there is never a gap
  before the right arm.
- **Small bracket marker** (`.eyebrow`, `.dl-eyebrow`, `.signup-eyebrow`,
  the lockup eyebrow): the section label in tracked capitals, and status
  lines in sentence case (`.eyebrow--status`). School-level markers split the
  bracket (teal left, indigo right); `.eyebrow--med` is teal only,
  `.eyebrow--tech` indigo only; on a block the marker takes the block accent.
- **The split hairline** (teal then indigo, hard stop) appears only at the
  very top of the page (header) and the top of the footer.

### Catalogue backbone

- **Programs** are a ruled, numbered list, not cards: number (Fraunces, teal),
  name and summary, hours in their own column, one outline button. Phones
  stack it beside the number. No carousel.
- **Facts** are label / value rows on hairlines under a 2px ink rule
  (enrollment facts, term schedule, information sessions).
- **Tuition** is a real table: 2px ink rules top and bottom, hairlines
  between rows, Med then Tech group rows, tabular numbers right-aligned.
- **Steps** ("How to enroll") are numbered 1 to 4 with large Fraunces
  numerals in the accent colour on a heavy rule. No circles, no cards.
- **Who qualifies and the module list** are plain typographic blocks (a
  Fraunces title over a short paragraph, on a heavy rule) on paper or the
  indigo tint, under a bracketed headline.

### Buttons and forms

- Buttons: solid, 4px radius, sentence case, weight 700, 48px tall. Med
  `.btn-primary` teal (`--med-ink`), Tech `.btn-tech` indigo
  (`--tech-deep-2`); secondary buttons are 2px outlines. Hover darkens. No
  shadow, no lift.
- Fields: white, 1px ink border, 4px radius, 16px text, 0.9rem by 1rem
  padding, at least 52px tall; focus is a 2px outline in the track colour.
  Forms sit on paper: inside a colour block on a `.panel-paper` sheet with a
  bracketed heading (the home and Digital Literacy interest forms, the
  contact form). Behaviour, field names and Turnstile wiring are unchanged.

### Never use

- Callouts with a coloured side bar; icon-in-pill chip lists; decorative
  icons before items or headings (icons stay only where they say what a thing
  is: phone, email, map pin, social logos, external link, form status).
- Glass, translucent panels, `backdrop-filter`, gradient blobs, sheens, glow
  rings, gradient text or badges, drop shadows on content, nested cards.
- Uppercase, letter-spaced or pill-shaped buttons.
- Centred section headers (the 404 page excepted).
- Illustrations, and stock photos beyond the licensed ones listed in
  `docs/image-credits.md`. The old Digital Literacy line drawing is gone (its
  place is a ruled list of the hero facts in Fraunces).
- Full-bleed photo heroes, photos as backgrounds behind text, and one photo
  per program card.
- A second motif. The bracket is the only one.

### Header

- **Masthead on paper**, 72px tall on desktop and 60px under 1081px, fixed.
  The split teal/indigo hairline runs along the top (indigo only on the
  Digital Literacy page); a 1px ink rule appears under it only once the page
  has scrolled, and fades in. No shadow, ever.
- **Logo left**, linked to the homepage of the language. It is the way home,
  so there is no "Home" link.
- **At most five links**, Plus Jakarta Sans 600. The home and program pages
  lead with the two tracks: Med, Tech, then Enroll and About (About covers
  the people; the footer still links to Faculty). Med and Tech carry a 3px
  underline in their track colour (deep teal, indigo) on hover and for the
  section in view; a program page keeps the Med underline on. The Digital
  Literacy page has its own four links (Who qualifies, What you learn, How to
  enroll, For caseworkers), all with the indigo underline, and never a Med
  link (NJDOL rule).
- **Right cluster:** "Student login" as a quiet text link with the external
  mark (it opens the class site), the language switch, then ONE button in
  the page's track: "Request info" in teal to the contact form on the home
  and Med pages, "Tell us you are interested" in indigo to `#interest` on the
  Digital Literacy page.
- **Language switch:** `[ EN ] ES`. The current language is filled in ink
  inside the small bracket; the other is a plain link. Each is a 44px
  target. No pill.
- **No phone number in the header** on any page: it lives in the footer and
  the contact section.
- **Under 1081px:** a "[ Menu ]" text button (48px) opens a full-screen
  drawer on ink (100dvh, scrolls on its own, no scroll chaining). The links
  are set large in Fraunces on 56px rows (Med in the teal tint, Tech in the
  indigo tint, the rest in paper), then the track button, Student login with
  its subtitle, and the language switch at the bottom. "[ Close ]" sits top
  right. Focus moves into the drawer and is trapped there, Escape closes it
  and returns focus to "[ Menu ]", and the page behind does not scroll.
  Closed, the drawer is hidden (`visibility: hidden`), so nothing of it shows
  or takes focus.

### Motion

- One easing (`--ease-out`, `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.7, 0.2, 1)`) and three
  durations: `--dur-fast` 160ms for hover and focus, `--dur-reveal` 280ms for
  reveals, `--dur-drawer` 320ms for the drawer opening (it closes in 200ms).
- Only opacity and transform animate; nothing animates width, height or
  layout. `will-change` only on the open drawer.
- Hover is colour and underline only: no lift, no scale, no shadow.
- Headings and blocks fade up 12px the first time they enter the viewport,
  once each (`ScrollReveal.astro`, one IntersectionObserver for the page).
  The framed headline in each hero draws its bracket in once (arms from 60%
  to full height). With JavaScript off everything is simply visible.
- The drawer fades in and its rows rise into place 40ms apart.
- `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` turns all of it off: state changes are
  instant and nothing moves.

### Photos

- **Small, beside type.** A 4:5 inset next to a headline or pitch, one 3:2
  image beside a section header, or a pair of small 4:5 photos. Never a
  full-bleed hero, never behind text, never one per program. An inset is at
  most 40% of its column on desktop and moves under the text on phones (the
  home Med inset switches to the 3:2 crop of the same photo there).
- **Frame.** Square corners at the 4px radius, a 1px ink hairline. On an
  indigo or teal block the photo sits on a paper mat (`frame="mat"`). No
  shadow, no gradient, no rounded blob, no tint over the photo.
- **The class site** is shown as a real screenshot (test account, no
  personal data) in a plain ink laptop (home Tech block) or phone (Digital
  Literacy page), and the sign-in page small in the "Already enrolled?" note.
- **Files.** `public/images/campus/` (our campus, graded warm and
  desaturated) and `public/images/tech/` (class-site screenshots and the
  licensed stock photo). Each is AVIF and WebP at the exported widths (480,
  960, 1440, never upscaled) plus a 24px `-lqip.jpg` placeholder, served by
  `src/components/Photo.astro` with `width`/`height`, `decoding="async"`,
  and `loading="lazy"` on everything but the first photo of the page.
- **People.** Stock models are never captioned or described as program
  participants or benefit recipients; alt text stays neutral ("An adult
  working on a laptop in a computer classroom"). Credits are in
  `docs/image-credits.md`.
- **Budget.** Images on a page stay under about 600 KB on a phone.

### Mobile first

Design and check at 375 and 390px first. 1rem gutters, 16px form text,
44px tap targets, no horizontal scroll, buttons full width on phones, the two
path blocks stacked Med then Tech.

## 3. Typography

| Use                          | Family              | Weight    |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------- | --------- |
| H1 to H3, catalogue entries, numerals | Fraunces   | 500 to 600 |
| Body / UI, H4 to H6, buttons | Plus Jakarta Sans   | 400 to 800 |
| Tabular numbers              | Plus Jakarta Sans, `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` | 600 to 800 |

### Scale

- **H1:** `clamp(2.6rem, 9vw, 6rem)`, line-height 0.98, letter-spacing -0.025em
- **H2 (section title):** `clamp(2.1rem, 5.2vw, 3.6rem)`, line-height 1.02
- **H3:** 1.375 to 3rem depending on the component (catalogue names 1.75rem)
- **Body:** 1rem / 1.6 line-height

## 15. Two languages, always

The site is published in English and Spanish. They are not two projects. They
are one project with two sets of words, and both sets are part of every change.

### The rule

**Every copy change is made in English and Spanish in the same change.** The
wording lives in `src/i18n/en.ts` and `src/i18n/es.ts`, and, for program
wording, in `src/data/programs.ts` as `L('English', 'Espanol')`. A change that
touches only one language is an unfinished change. `npm run check:i18n` must
pass before anything is published, and the build itself stops if a key exists
in one language and not the other.

URLs: English at `/`, `/digital-literacy/`, `/programs/<id>/`. Spanish at the
same paths under `/es/`. The language switch in the header links to the same
page in the other language. Nothing redirects by browser language.

### Spanish voice

Same voice rules as English, in Spanish:

- **usted**, never **tu**.
- Short sentences. Plain words. The word a person would use out loud.
- Respect first. Never imply the reader does not know things, is behind, or
  needs reassurance. No "no se preocupe", "es facil", "aunque no sepa nada".
  Frame the skills check as placing each person at the right level, never as
  help for beginners.
- No exclamation marks, in either direction: no `!` and no `¡`.
- No em dashes and no en dashes.
- Not a literal translation of the English. Say the same thing the way it is
  said in Spanish in New Jersey.

### The program name in Spanish

**Digital Literacy Training is "Capacitación en Habilidades Digitales" in
Spanish, everywhere a reader sees it.** On first use on a page it carries the
official English name: "Capacitación en Habilidades Digitales (Digital Literacy
Training)". Where the slot is tight (a chip, a badge, a button, a `<title>`)
the short form is "Habilidades Digitales".

**"Alfabetización" is never used for this program.** In Latin American Spanish
"alfabetización" is the word for teaching an adult to read and write
("campañas de alfabetización"). To an adult on TANF, "Capacitación en
Alfabetización Digital" reads as *training for people who cannot read*, which
is exactly what the respect-first rule in section 1 forbids. "Habilidades
digitales" is the standard US workforce term, says the same thing, and does not
put a deficit on the reader. The only place "alfabetizacion digital" survives is
`home.seo.keywords` / `dlt.seo.keywords`, because that is what people type into
a search box and the tag is not something anyone reads.

If NJDOL ever requires a specific Spanish name for the program, theirs wins and
this section gets rewritten.

**Punctuation in Spanish.** No serial (Oxford) comma before the final `y` / `e`
of an enumeration: that is an English rule and the RAE does not use it. The one
exception is an enumeration whose own items contain `y`, where the comma is what
keeps it readable ("El mouse y el teclado, los archivos y las carpetas, y cómo
cambiar la configuración"). A comma before `y` joining two full clauses is not a
serial comma and stays.

listo", "no está solo", "usted mismo") assume a man. Use a phrasing that does
not: "Quiere capacitarse", "Aquí no está por su cuenta", "avise usted",
"por su cuenta", "¿Tiene dudas?".
